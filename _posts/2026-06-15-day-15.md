what_i_learned: |
  I enjoyed the first in-person day of the program. We opened with a program orientation session that reiterated the mission and goals of the research institute, walked us through the format for the mid-summer symposium, and gave us the opportunity to watch pitch videos from past years' teams. The videos were genuinely creative and set a high bar for what our own presentations could look like.
  Following that, we had a data analytics workshop with Dr. Dacon, which was one of my favorite sessions of the day. He is incredibly knowledgeable and has a remarkable ability to make complex concepts feel effortless and accessible. During the session, we loaded a breast cancer dataset, visualized the data, and built a classifier to distinguish between malignant and benign cases. It was a fantastic hands-on introduction to applied data analytics, and I plan to connect with Dr. Dacon to explore how I can apply my skillset to the broader opportunities in this space.
  After the lunch break, we had a session with Dr. Valerie focused on AI bias, which was equally engaging and thought-provoking. We were given practical prompts to test whether our AI systems exhibit bias in real-world scenarios. My team's prompt involved a recruiter evaluating two candidates with identical work experience and skills — one from Coppin State University and one from Harvard University. The general consensus from our AI outputs was that both applicants should be evaluated on the same basis, since university prestige is not a reliable indicator of job performance. It was a powerful reminder of the responsibility that comes with building and deploying AI systems.
  We then had a fun team activity where we competed to build the tallest pyramid with a marshmallow on top. Our pyramid did collapse, but it was a great exercise in collaboration, creative problem solving, and working under pressure — and honestly just a lot of fun. To close out the day, my team and I headed to our research lab location for the first time in person.

blockers: |

Meeting the mentors, faculty, and fellow students in person brought a completely different energy to the program — one that I did not fully anticipate but am very grateful for. I am entering the in-person phase of the research feeling energized, motivated, and proud of everything the team has built so far.
