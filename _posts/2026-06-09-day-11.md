what_i_learned: |
  Today's schedule was split between a productive research lab session and an insightful literature review with Dr. Pandey. In the lab, I focused on building and executing the data pipeline for the BIDMC dataset. I successfully constructed a specialized BIDMC loader to read synchronized ECG, PPG, and RESP signals from each record and segment them into fixed windows. Since both BIDMC and MIMIC-III sample at 125 Hz, this windowing logic will transfer seamlessly later. From there, I implemented the full three-signal preprocessing pipeline, applying specific bandpass filters (0.5–40 Hz for ECG, 0.5–8 Hz for PPG, and 0.1–1.0 Hz for RESP) followed by normalization, and generated comparative plots for the raw, filtered, and normalized states. Finally, I extracted the mandatory 6-domain dictionary for CFD (Time, Frequency, and Time-Frequency for both ECG and PPG), purposefully excluding the respiratory signal as it is reserved strictly for the trust gate architecture. 
  Later, during the literature review session with Dr. Pandey, we engaged in hands-on, practical learning. A major takeaway was learning how to efficiently and correctly prompt AI models—not just to generate better outputs, but to formulate questions that provoke deeper critical thinking and refine our research methodology.

blockers: |
  While assembling the multi-signal preprocessing pipeline and mapping out the feature domains, I ran into a few data pipeline bugs. Navigating the data shapes and filtering constraints across three distinct biosignals required careful debugging, but I was able to successfully isolate the issues and correct the pipeline logic before the end of the lab session.

reflection: |
  Transitioning from raw biosignals to a structured, filtered, 6-domain dictionary really highlighted how critical precise data engineering is before any fusion takes place. Balancing that technical implementation with the prompt engineering strategies discussed with Dr. Pandey made today a fantastic reminder that the way we frame our questions—whether to an AI or within our own research framework—profoundly shapes the quality of our results.
