what_i_learned: |
  Today began with our research lab session, but instead of immediately diving into coding, my team and I focused on creating our project pitch video. We were able to capture all of the footage we needed well ahead of schedule, which gave us enough time to review our shots and make sure we were satisfied with the final result. I really enjoyed the filming process—it was a fun change of pace from coding and writing, and it felt like we were filming a commercial.
  After returning to the research lab, I continued working through our research manual by beginning Experiment 3: CFD Contribution to Reliable Prediction. Unlike Experiment 1, which focused on identifying complementary feature domains, and Experiment 2, which focused on benign and non-benign drift classification, Experiment 3 evaluates whether selecting CFD-optimal domain pairs actually leads to more reliable predictions. I spent time studying the experimental design, which compares three different feature fusion strategies: the CFD-optimal domain pair identified in Experiment 1, non-CFD (redundant) domain pairs that fail the complementarity criteria, and a model that uses all six ECG and PPG domains together.
  I also learned about the primary evaluation metric for this experiment, the Complementarity Reliability Gain (CRG), which measures the improvement in Drift Classification Accuracy (DCA) achieved by the CFD-optimal domains over the non-complementary domains. The objective is to demonstrate that selecting complementary feature domains not only improves fusion performance but also produces more stable and reliable predictions under physiological drift. Understanding the motivation behind this experiment helped me see how the previous experiments connect together into a complete framework for evaluating multimodal biosignal representations.
