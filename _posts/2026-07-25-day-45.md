what_i_learned: |
  Today was the first day of the National Symposium on Effective and Ethical AI. After registering for the event, I joined the opening session, which began with remarks from Dr. Natasha Dartigue.
  The opening remarks were followed by a keynote presentation from Dr. Gilbert titled “Equitable AI in the Post-SFFA SCOTUS Decision in Admissions.” It was interesting to learn about the notable work he has completed and continues to pursue at the intersection of artificial intelligence and education. His presentation encouraged me to think more deeply about how AI systems can affect access, fairness, and opportunity within educational institutions.
  After the keynote session, the SAIRI participants gathered to take group photographs. I later attended the information session for the hackathon taking place during the symposium. Although I had not originally planned to participate, I decided to join and was able to find a team.
  My team chose to focus on an important challenge in education. We are developing an idea that we believe could be useful to teachers and, in turn, positively affect their students. Today reminded me that being open to unexpected opportunities can lead to new experiences, collaborations, and opportunities to apply what I have learned.

blockers: |
  One challenge is that my team has a limited amount of time to develop and present our hackathon solution. We need to clearly define the problem, divide responsibilities, create a practical solution, and prepare a strong presentation before tomorrow.
  I also need to balance the hackathon with my preparation for the final research presentation. Both activities require focus and practice, so I will need to manage my time carefully and make sure that I continue reviewing my assigned symposium slides.
  As a team, we must ensure that our hackathon idea is not only creative but also realistic, helpful, and clearly connected to the needs of teachers and students.

reflection: |
  Today was an exciting start to the symposium. I enjoyed listening to the keynote presentation and learning more about the relationship between artificial intelligence, education, and equity. The sessions showed me that technical innovation should always be considered alongside its possible effects on people and communities.
  Participating in the hackathon was an unexpected decision, but I am glad that I chose to take part. Finding a team and working on an educational challenge gave me another opportunity to collaborate, think creatively, and apply technology to a real-world problem. My team hopes to develop a meaningful solution and win the hackathon tomorrow.
  I will also continue preparing for my final research presentation. I am excited to share what my team and I have accomplished throughout the program, although it is bittersweet to know that the experience is coming to an end. This program has been insightful and rewarding, and as every good thing must come to an end, I hope to finish it with confidence, gratitude, and a strong final presentation.
