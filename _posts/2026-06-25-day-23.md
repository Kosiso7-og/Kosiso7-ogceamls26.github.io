what_i_learned: |
  Today's morning research lab session started with a clear focus to lock in properly, especially with the mid-summer symposium fast approaching. To make our multi-modal architecture completely robust, I pivoted away from the standard .hea dataset readers to build a custom BIDMCCSVLoader that streams raw physiological data directly from the _Signals.csv files. More importantly, I executed a critical course-correction on our baseline classification targets: instead of using proxy respiratory states, I mapped my 3,180 signal windows to true clinical Cardiovascular Disease (CVD) ground truth labels — Normal Controls vs. Abnormal Anomalies — to bring the notebook into full compliance with Dr. Oladunni's PECS Lab Manual guidelines.

  After running the full pyCFD 3.0 Same-Encoder Discovery Matrix, I observed high information redundancy clustering across the feature spaces. To match this fresh low-range data profile, I re-engineered my entire 5-figure visualization suite to dynamically scale boundaries and text offsets, completely eliminating the overlapping text and scattered collisions from earlier runs. Finally, to ensure my pipeline can handle shifting optimal pairs on a rerun, I built a fully automated, dynamic patient-stratified split engine that parses report.recommended_strategy to slice zero-leakage training tensors on the fly.

  Later in the day, Dr. Mack, Elijah, and Michael came to the lab to check in on us. It was genuinely refreshing to have them stop by — they spent time asking for our suggestions for the next connect event, asked how our individual research work was going, and took time to listen. That kind of direct, supportive engagement from the leadership team left me feeling more uplifted and motivated. We then continued working in the lab, and I was appointed the unofficial DJ for the final stretch of the day, playing some of my favorite songs while we worked. Today was a fun and progressive day.

blockers: |
