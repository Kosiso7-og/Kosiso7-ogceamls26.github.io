what_i_learned: |
  Today's morning session kicked off with the research lab, where I focused on evaluating a comprehensive set of theoretical questions shared with us by our faculty mentor regarding our cross-modal Complementary Feature Domain (CFD) discovery framework. I systematically evaluated 15 distinct pairwise feature combinations across Time, Frequency, and Time-Frequency spaces for both ECG and PPG signals. By running these experiments, I successfully mapped how domain complementarity shifts under external stress and validated our core hypothesis regarding relationship stability under perturbation.

  I also completed a detailed performance evaluation of our Level 1 PECS Trust Hierarchy. We uncovered a fascinating phenomenon where the single-channel PPG Time-Frequency modality classifier achieved a high localized accuracy for identifying signal corruption, yet its global diagnostic classification accuracy dropped significantly. This proves that while the encoder accurately flags structural signal modifications caused by localized drift scenarios such as a cold hand scenario, it cannot inherently distinguish technical sensor artifacts from true physiological crises on its own. This divergence perfectly validates our Trust Hierarchy framework, which safely isolates the fault and triggers a cross-modal disagreement flag to halt erroneous clinical predictions.

  Once I completed the faculty mentor tasks, I transitioned into building and refining my slides for the weekly showcase, incorporating the findings and progress from the research lab session into a clear and structured presentation.

  Following that, my team and I presented our current progress in front of two other research groups in the program and their graduate mentor. This presentation felt incredibly helpful as a dry run for the upcoming mid-summer symposium on Monday. Presenting in front of this smaller group of peers was a comfortable experience, and it gave me a strong boost of confidence that I hope to carry right into Monday's major presentation. I really liked that we received a lot of engaging questions from the audience — the feedback helped us identify exactly which elements of our slides need clearer explanations to ensure easier understandability for a broader audience.
  
  To close out the day, our entire cohort gathered for an insightful town hall session with Dr. Kofi Nyarko.

Even though weak modalities like PPG perform poorly in isolation, they can possess highly stable latent structures under external stress. When paired with an orthogonal partner domain, they provide a noise-resistant latent topology that allows the shared projection layer to robustly resolve complex clinical drift. With our pipeline validated, I am looking forward to carrying this momentum straight into the symposium on Monday!
