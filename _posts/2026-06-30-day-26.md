what_i_learned: |
  Today was our highly anticipated Mid-Summer Symposium, marking a major milestone for our research cohorts. Heading into the day, I actually was not feeling nervous — my practice run on Friday in front of my peers from the other groups had gone well, leaving me confident in our multi-modal fusion architecture. The only piece of constructive feedback I had received from that rehearsal was to refine my time management, so I felt prepared to execute. However, about an hour before my team was scheduled to present, a wave of presentation anxiety hit me. Watching the other teams speak with such seamless, articulate confidence on stage caused me to second-guess myself, triggering the presentation nerves I have normally struggled with.

  When our turn arrived, I pushed through the self-doubt and delivered my sections of the pipeline narrative. Looking back immediately after the presentation, I know I did not perform badly at all. I managed to communicate our core technical mechanics — from the 2D time-frequency scalograms to our shared projection layers — without letting the anxiety take over. That said, the experience highlighted clear room for improvement regarding my pacing and overall presentation skills. To cap off the event, we received incredibly positive feedback from Dr. Mack, who specifically commended the substantial research progress teams has made over the course of the program.

blockers: |
  While no technical pipeline blockers occurred today, navigating acute presentation anxiety right before taking the stage was a significant mental hurdle. Pushing through that nervous window allowed me to complete the delivery successfully, though it remains an area of active focus going forward.

reflection: |
  Today was an invaluable exercise in stepping outside my comfort zone. Presentation anxiety is a very real challenge for me, but getting up in front of a much larger, formal audience and successfully articulating our machine learning framework was a massive step toward building long-term confidence.

  Hearing Dr. Mack recognize the depth of our progress was an incredibly rewarding way to close out the symposium. It validated the late hours we spent debugging encoders and handling sensor drift. I am grateful for the platform to present today, and I am eager to keep refining my public speaking skills as we move into the final chapters of our summer research.
