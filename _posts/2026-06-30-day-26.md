what_i_learned: |
  Today was an exciting change of pace as I attended the AWS Summit in Washington, D.C. After checking in, I caught the keynote session led by Dave Levy, Vice President of Worldwide Public Sector at AWS, which focused on securing cloud infrastructure for artificial intelligence and government workloads. He brought out two guest speakers who provided fascinating sector-specific insights. The first speaker, from the energy sector, countered the common rhetoric regarding the environmental impact of data centers. He noted that their consumption of water is comparatively small relative to the societal benefits they yield. Because water is a renewable resource, this perspective made me realize I need to dive deeper into the actual environmental trade-offs, as it is a frequent point of public debate. The second speaker introduced KoraAI, an AI agent platform that immediately caught my attention as a highly promising tool for building future production-level projects.

  Following the keynote, I participated in a one-hour hands-on lab focused on building production-ready Generative AI applications on AWS. While the short duration meant the scope differed slightly from my initial expectations, it was still a highly valuable technical overview. I walked away with a better functional understanding of how core AWS services — specifically Amazon DynamoDB for NoSQL data storage, Amazon S3 for scalable object storage, and AWS Lambda for serverless backend computing — integrate to support AI pipelines. I wrapped up the day at the Expo floor, exploring booths from various tech companies, learning about unfamiliar software ecosystems, and even grabbing a picture with the Snowflake mascot, which was a fun highlight.

blockers: |
  The primary constraint today was time management within a fast-paced summit environment. A one-hour technical lab is inherently limited in depth, making it difficult to fully explore complex architecture deployments on-site. However, it provided a solid foundational blueprint that I can unpack and replicate independently later.

reflection: |
  Attending the summit was incredibly refreshing and served as a great reminder of how rapidly the broader cloud and AI ecosystems are evolving. Stepping out of the academic research space and into a massive industry event provided excellent exposure to enterprise-grade tools.

  Seeing how companies are leveraging serverless functions and managed databases to scale generative AI models bridges the gap between theoretical machine learning and real-world implementation. I am leaving the event highly motivated to experiment with the tools I discovered today — especially KoraAI and AWS serverless architecture — as I continue expanding my engineering toolkit.
