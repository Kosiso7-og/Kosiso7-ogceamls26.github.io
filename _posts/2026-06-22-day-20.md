what_i_learned: |
  Coming back from the long weekend, today had a good mix of administrative tasks, skill-building, and a meaningful research breakthrough. We kicked off the morning by getting guidance on filling out our timesheets, which I was able to complete.

  From there, we moved into an Overleaf practice session that Dr. Mack had assigned prior to the break. I will be honest — like several of my cohort members, I did not get it done over the weekend. However, with the guidance of Mr. Michael and a cohort member, Destiny, I was able to work through it and get everything completed during the session. We practiced citing references, inserting images, creating lists using enumeration, applying bold and italic formatting, and building tables. It was a practical and hands-on way to get comfortable with Overleaf ahead of writing our research paper.

  After the Overleaf practice, we played a game of Taboo consisting of two groups. My team did not win, but it was genuinely one of the most fun activities we have done so far. I was not the one giving descriptions for my team, and watching others try to get their teammates to guess words without using the restricted terms made me realize how challenging that role actually is.

  The afternoon research lab session was particularly productive and marked a turning point for my progress this week. Our faculty mentor joined us and individually reviewed where each of us stood and discuss the path forward. Given the number of hiccups I had encountered with my previous code over the past week — largely due to its growing complexity — my faculty mentor supported my decision to start a fresh code file to perform my experiments individually and in a more structured way. This was exactly the reset I needed. In the new file, I have already successfully loaded my dataset, extracted the necessary files for preprocessing, and identified the optimal domain pairs for my CFD multi-modality analysis. My next steps are to begin preprocessing the dataset, build the encoders, and implement the perturbations.

blockers: |
  No significant blockers today. The decision to start a new, cleaner code file has already removed the main source of friction from last week. I am feeling much more confident about the week ahead with a clearer, more structured path through my experiments.

reflection: |
  Today felt like a genuine fresh start, both coming back from the long weekend and in my research work. Starting a new code file might seem like a step backward on the surface, but it was actually the most productive decision I have made in the past week.

  It was also encouraging to see how much I was able to accomplish in the research lab session today compared to some of the more frustrating days last week. The Overleaf session was a great reminder that it is never too late to get something done, and I am grateful to Mr. Michael and Destiny for helping me work through it. Looking forward to a strong and productive week ahead.
