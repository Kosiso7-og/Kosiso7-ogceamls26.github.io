what_i_learned: |
    Today began with our research lab session, where I continued working with the MIMIC dataset and expanded the scale of my pyCFD experiment. I increased the number of signal segments from 2,000 to 5,000 so that I could evaluate whether a larger sample size would affect the stability and outcome of the domain-pair analysis. As expected, the increased number of segments also caused the code to take significantly longer to run because the model had more ECG and PPG data to process, train on, and evaluate.
    One of the most important observations from today's experiment was that the optimal domain pair changed when I increased the dataset size. With 2,000 segments, the strongest pair was ECG Time-Frequency and PPG Time. However, after running the analysis with 5,000 segments, the strongest pair became ECG Time and PPG Time. The ECG_Time and PPG_Time pair achieved the highest CFD index and fusion gain, which suggests that the raw temporal structures of the two signals provided the strongest complementary information at the larger sample size. This result reminded me that experimental conclusions can change when the amount of data changes and that larger datasets can sometimes produce more reliable estimates of model performance and feature complementarity.
    After completing the expanded pyCFD analysis, I began moving forward with the additional experiments described in our research manual. I focused specifically on understanding Experiment 2, which involves benign and non-benign drift classification. This experiment requires generating clinically motivated perturbations in the ECG and PPG signals, training modality-specific encoders, and measuring how the models respond to changes in the signals. I also began organizing the steps required to calculate the evaluation metrics listed in the manual, including drift classification accuracy, embedding-drift correlation, stability violation rate, and ECG–PPG disagreement cases.

blockers: |
    My main blocker today was a technical issue with my laptop. At one point, the computer became unresponsive, and my mouse stopped moving completely. Since my laptop has a touchscreen, I was still able to continue some of my work by navigating directly on the screen. However, tasks that required precise mouse control, especially manually selecting, copying, and pasting large sections of code, became much more difficult and slowed down my progress.
    Another challenge was the increased computational cost of using 5,000 signal segments. The larger experiment required much more processing time than the earlier 2,000-segment version, which meant I had to wait longer before I could review the results or continue to the next stage. This reinforced the importance of testing code on smaller subsets before committing to a full experiment and saving intermediate results so that completed work does not need to be rerun unnecessarily.

reflection: |
    Today's work showed me how strongly dataset size can influence both computational performance and experimental outcomes. Although ECG Time-Frequency and PPG Time appeared to be the strongest pair in the smaller experiment, the 5,000-segment run identified ECG Time and PPG Time as the most complementary pair. This does not necessarily mean that the earlier result was incorrect, but it demonstrates why experiments need to be repeated at a larger scale before drawing final conclusions. It also gave me more confidence in interpreting CFD index values, fusion gain, statistical significance, and domain rankings.
Beginning Experiment 2 also helped me recognize the difference between feature-domain discovery and drift evaluation. Experiment 1 identifies which signal representations work best together, while Experiment 2 uses those selected representations to determine whether a signal change is benign or potentially clinically significant. Since ECG_Time and PPG_Time were selected from the larger MIMIC experiment, those domains will guide the design of the ECG and PPG encoders used in the next phase.
    Despite the laptop problems, I was still able to make meaningful progress by adapting and using the touchscreen to continue working. At the end of the day, I plan to investigate the mouse and responsiveness issue so that it does not interfere with future experiments. Overall, today was productive because I completed a larger-scale domain-pair analysis, gained a clearer understanding of how sample size affects the results, and began preparing the implementation for benign and non-benign drift classification.
