what_i_learned: |
  Today was the final day of the National Symposium on Effective and Ethical AI and the day of the undergraduate research presentations. My team presented our research before the lunch break, which I appreciated because I was able to relax and enjoy lunch afterward without feeling anxious about the presentation.
  Presenting our work gave us the opportunity to share what we had learned and accomplished throughout the program. It also allowed me to practice communicating information to an audience and working with my teammates to deliver a connected presentation.
  I also presented our hackathon pitch with my team members. We were able to clearly explain the problem we wanted to address, the idea behind our project, and how the application could support teachers and students. Although we did not win the hackathon, the experience taught me the value of collaborating with new people, developing an idea within a limited amount of time, and confidently presenting it to judges.
  Today reminded me that completing a project is not only about the final result. It is also about the knowledge, skills, relationships, and experiences gained throughout the process.

blockers: |
  One of my main challenges today was managing my nervousness before the research presentation. I wanted to make sure that I explained my slides clearly, remembered the important details, and supported my teammates during our presentation.
  I also had to balance the final research presentation with preparing for the hackathon pitch. Both required confidence, clear communication, and the ability to respond to questions from the audience or judges.
  Although our hackathon team did not win, I recognize that not every project will receive an award. The experience was still valuable because it allowed me to work with new people, explore an important problem in education, and practice developing and presenting a solution within a short period.

reflection: |
  The day ended with an award ceremony recognizing participants in different symposium events, including the poster presentations, spotlight videos, and hackathon. It was exciting to celebrate the work and accomplishments of everyone who participated.
  I am very happy to have completed this program and to be leaving with new knowledge, meaningful connections, and new friendships. I truly appreciate the opportunities that participating in this program has given me, both academically and personally.
  I am especially grateful to my faculty mentors, graduate mentors, Dr. Mack, Michael, and Elijah for the different forms of guidance and assistance they provided and for the time they invested in working with us throughout the program. Their support contributed greatly to my learning and overall experience.
  Although it is bittersweet that the program has come to an end, I am proud of everything my team and I accomplished. This experience strengthened my research, teamwork, presentation, and problem-solving skills. I hope to carry the lessons, relationships, and confidence I gained from this program into future academic and professional opportunities.
