what_i_learned: |
    Today was spent entirely back in the research lab, shifting my focus toward a rigorous literature review to build a strong theoretical foundation for our research paper. I dedicated the session to evaluating and consolidating core reference papers that map the precise structural evolution of machine learning in digital cardiology. Specifically, I analyzed and synthesized five benchmark studies to trace how the field is transitioning from traditional, handcrafted machine learning algorithms to end-to-end deep neural networks.

Through this process, I gained a much clearer structural understanding of the current translational constraints in the field—particularly how historical reliance on intra-patient validation paradigms can mask model generalization errors when systems face real-world patient data shift. I worked on synthesizing these findings into a unified narrative, detailing everything from algorithmic validation flaws (such as the widespread over-reliance on the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database) to edge hardware limitations on mobile telemetry platforms, and finally, real-world bedside deployment yields like improving the positive predictive value of diagnostic echocardiograms. This exercise was incredibly valuable for articulating exactly where our own multi-modal framework fills critical gaps in the existing literature.
