  Today began with a research lab session where I worked on the literature assignment that Dr. Pandey had assigned during the previous session. This provided an opportunity to revisit several research papers and strengthen my understanding of how to critically evaluate scientific literature. Later, we joined the literature review session, which focused on practical research communication skills. During this session, we learned about transitions and signal phrases, both of which are essential for creating coherent and well-structured academic writing. We also learned the LOCK framework for reading papers and articles more effectively, as well as the PEACCC framework for organizing and communicating ideas in a clear and persuasive manner.
  
  We had an engaging activity involving asking ChatGPT to describe our character based on our previous conversations. It was interesting to see how AI systems can identify recurring patterns, interests, and personality traits from long-term interactions. The exercise highlighted both the capabilities of modern AI systems and the importance of maintaining thoughtful and consistent communication.
  
  After the literature review session, I returned to the research lab and continued working on the BIDMC biosignal processing pipeline. During this process, I discovered that several bugs remained in my preprocessing and feature extraction code. I systematically debugged the pipeline, corrected data handling issues, and successfully generated visualizations for the Cross-Domain Fusion (CFD) Index, fusion gain, and multimodal heatmaps. These visualizations provided valuable insight into the relationships between ECG and PPG domains. I then analyzed the results to identify the CFD-optimal domain subset, which will serve as the foundation for the next stage of the project. With this analysis complete, I am now prepared to begin developing the PECS encoders for multimodal representation learning.
